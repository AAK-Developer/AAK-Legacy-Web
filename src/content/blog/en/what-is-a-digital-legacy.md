A digital legacy is not simply a list of accounts or passwords. It is the collection of documents, instructions, memories, and decisions that explain an important part of your life and that somebody else may need to understand one day.

## Context creates value

An isolated file may be useless when nobody knows why it exists, whether it remains current, or what should happen to it. Preparing a legacy means adding that context: what it is, why it matters, who it is for, and when it should be delivered.

AAK Legacy organizes this into five spaces: Family, Digital Life, Assets, Emotional Legacy, and Private. The separation keeps practical information distinct from memories and especially sensitive content.

## Start with one thing

Choose something that only you know how to find or explain today: a document, a contact, a short instruction, or a personal message. Your Legacy can grow calmly over time.

## Preparation can remain private

Content is encrypted on your device before storage. Guardians help verify but cannot read your Legacy. Recipients receive only what you assigned to them.

Preparing a digital legacy is not about anticipating absence. It is about making sure important information does not depend forever on your memory.
